1. Executive Summary
This Product Requirements Document (PRD) outlines the system specifications and technical
pathways for a state-of-the-art Driver Drowsiness Detection System. As driver fatigue remains a
leading cause of traffic accidents worldwide, this project aims to develop a robust, multi-layered
monitoring platform.
By synergizing non-intrusive observation techniques with advanced physiological data and vehicle-
based telematics, the system will accurately evaluate alertness in real-time, proactively alerting the
driver and potentially triggering automated safety protocols to prevent accidents.
2. Technical Monitoring Pathways
To establish a highly reliable evaluation of driver alertness, the system integrates three distinct
technical pathways. This multi-layered approach ensures redundancy and minimizes false positives
by corroborating data across behavioral, mechanical, and physiological domains.
2.1 Behavioral Monitoring
This non-intrusive pathway utilizes in-cabin infrared (IR) cameras and machine learning-driven
computer vision to actively map driver facial cues and head dynamics regardless of ambient
lighting conditions.
Eye and Eyelid Tracking
Monitors blink frequency, gaze shifts away from the windshield, and prolonged eye closure
duration (PERCLOS). These metrics are the primary leading indicators utilized to spot fading
attention spans and micro-sleep events.
Yawning and Facial Cues
Tracks mouth opening metrics, jaw movements, and overall facial contortions. By analyzing
these cues, the system identifies frequent or sustained yawning as an early, pre-critical sign of
physical fatigue.

Head Posture
Measures head inclination and sudden tilting angles. The computer vision model maps the
driver's head posture over time to detect gradual drooping or the sudden dropping motion
characteristic of a driver nodding off.
2.2 Vehicle-Based Monitoring
This pathway analyzes the vehicle's dynamic behavior and the driver's interaction with operational
controls. By evaluating how the vehicle is being driven, the system can infer the cognitive state and
responsiveness of the operator.
Steering Patterns
Analyzes micro-corrections and steering wheel inputs via integrated electric power steering
(EPS) sensors. A marked decrease in normal micro-corrections, followed by sudden, erratic
steering inputs, is highly correlated with drowsy driving behavior.
Lane Position
Utilizes forward-facing Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) cameras to constantly
monitor road lane markings. It detects dangerous driving patterns such as sudden vehicle
drifting, weaving, or failure to maintain a consistent lane center over time.
2.3 Physiological Measurements
For applications requiring maximum precision (such as commercial trucking or autonomous vehicle
hand-off phases), the system incorporates direct, intrusive biometric tracking to gauge core
physiological fatigue levels.

Brain and Heart Activity
Relies on advanced wearable devices or sensors deeply integrated into the seat and steering
wheel. Key tracking parameters include:
Electroencephalogram (EEG): Monitors brain waves to detect the neurological transition
from wakefulness to drowsiness (e.g., shifts from beta to theta waves).
Electrocardiogram (ECG) & Heart Rate: Records heart rate variability (HRV) and ECG
signals, which demonstrate specific deterioration patterns as the body's autonomic nervous
system succumbs to fatigue.
3. Intervention Protocol
Upon detection of drowsiness through the aforementioned pathways, the system must employ a
graduated escalation matrix to regain driver focus safely.
Escalation Tiers
Tier 1 (Early Warning - Yawns, Minor Drifts): Gentle visual notification on the digital
cluster and subtle haptic feedback (seat or steering wheel vibration).
Tier 2 (Moderate Fatigue - Eyelid drooping, Erratic steering): Prominent auditory
chimes, tightening of the seatbelt, and mandatory dashboard prompts to take a rest break.
Tier 3 (Critical Danger - Micro-sleep, Severe drifting): High-decibel continuous alarms,
automated hazard light activation, and triggering of the vehicle's Emergency Stop Assist
(ESA) to safely pull the vehicle over.
4. Core Requirements & Constraints
Processing Latency: All image recognition (Behavioral) and telemetry processing (Vehicle-
based) must occur on the local edge-compute module with a total latency of less than 100ms.
Data Privacy: All raw video feeds and physiological telemetry must be processed locally and
overwritten continuously. No raw biometric data shall be transmitted to external servers without
explicit, opt-in consent from the user.
